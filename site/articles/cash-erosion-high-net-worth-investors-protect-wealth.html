Cash erosion: how high-net-worth investors can protect wealth

Cash feels safe because its number never goes down. But its value does. At even moderate inflation, a large cash balance quietly loses purchasing power every single year — a guaranteed, compounding loss that never shows up on a statement.

The scale of the problem

High-net-worth investors often hold far more cash than they intend to — proceeds from an exit, dividends waiting to be reinvested, a “temporary” buffer that becomes permanent. Held over a decade, an uninvested balance can silently forfeit a fifth or more of its real value. Doing nothing is not a neutral decision; it is a position, and historically a losing one.

Safety and return are not opposites

Protecting wealth from erosion doesn’t require reckless risk. The toolkit sophisticated investors use is well established:

  • Tiered liquidity — decide what cash is truly needed near-term, and put the rest on a spectrum from money-market yield to long-term strategies.
  • Diversified portfolios — spreading capital across asset classes, geographies and strategies so no single risk dominates.
  • Private markets — venture capital and private equity, where illiquidity is compensated with long-run return potential.
  • Active cash management — even the “safe” tier should earn competitive yield across several institutions and products.
